Caption — Facebook:
Super El Niño is here. Is your family ready for the heat? 🌡️

Researchers at the University of Chicago's Climate Impact Lab project about 31,400 more heat-related deaths in Nigeria between September 2026 and February 2027, compared with an average year. That's the highest projected toll of any country in their study. It's a model projection, not a count, and it hasn't been peer-reviewed yet. But it's a warning worth hearing.

What is El Niño? A natural warming of the Pacific Ocean that shifts weather around the world. This one is unusually strong: the US weather agency NOAA gives more than an 83% chance that a strong to very strong El Niño lasts through March 2027.

Who to look out for: people over 65, babies and young children, people with heart, lung or kidney conditions, outdoor workers, and anyone who lives alone.

5 heat-smart habits:
1. Drink water all day: 2 to 3 litres.
2. Skip hard work in the hottest hours and find shade.
3. Open windows at night; close them and block the sun by day.
4. Wear light, loose clothes and take cool showers.
5. Never leave a child in a parked car, not even for a minute.

Danger signs: confusion, fainting, or hot, dry skin. That's an emergency. Get medical help fast and cool the person down.

Who will you check on this week? Follow NiMet for heat updates in your state.

### Sun 2026-10-25, 10:00 WAT · LinkedIn · Climate, explained locally
Hook: Nigeria faces the highest projected heat death toll of this super El Niño.
Visual: `visuals/elnino-02.png` (the lime "31,400" slide), or the full carousel as a PDF.

Caption — LinkedIn:
Nigeria faces the highest projected heat death toll of this super El Niño.

The University of Chicago's Climate Impact Lab projects about 31,400 additional heat-related deaths in Nigeria between September 2026 and February 2027, compared with an average year. Their global figure is 451,000.

Two caveats matter. It's a model projection, not a count. And the method hasn't yet been published in a peer-reviewed journal.

The researchers call this heat a "postcard from the future": close to the temperatures climate change is projected to bring in about 20 years.

The people most at risk are older people, young children, people with heart, lung or kidney conditions, outdoor workers, and people in homes without cooling.

The study points to measures that help: heat warnings, outreach to vulnerable groups, cooling centres, protections for outdoor workers and stronger health services. Many of them sit with employers, schools, health workers and community groups, not just with governments.

What is your organisation doing to protect staff and communities from heat this season?

Study: https://impactlab.org/research/451000-at-risk/

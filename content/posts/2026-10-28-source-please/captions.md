Caption — Instagram:
Source, please. 🔍 3 checks before you share a climate stat:

1. Who said it? Find the original study, agency or report, not a screenshot of a screenshot.
2. Count or projection? "Deaths" and "projected deaths" are not the same thing.
3. When and where? A photo from another year or country can look like today.

We ran the same checks on the El Niño figure we shared last week. Swipe to see what we found.

Can't check it? Don't share it yet. 🌱

It's Global Media and Information Literacy Week (24 to 31 Oct).

Caption — Facebook:
Source, please. 🔍

A climate number lands in the family WhatsApp group. Before you forward it, run 3 checks:

1. Who said it? Find the original: the study, the agency, the report. Not a screenshot of a screenshot.
2. Is it a count or a projection? "Deaths" and "projected deaths" are not the same thing. Read the small print.
3. When and where? Check the date and the place. A photo from another year or another country can look like today.

We ran these checks on the figure we shared last week: about 31,400 extra heat-related deaths projected in Nigeria from September 2026 to February 2027. Who said it: the University of Chicago's Climate Impact Lab. Count or projection: a projection of extra deaths compared with an average year, not a count. When and where: Nigeria, Sept 2026 to Feb 2027.

If you can't check it, don't share it yet. 🌱

It's Global Media and Information Literacy Week (24 to 31 Oct).

The study: https://impactlab.org/research/451000-at-risk/
